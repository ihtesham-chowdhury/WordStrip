# WordStrip 0.15.0

Six themes instead of seven, each genuinely its own design — and a bar that sizes itself to the text you are
writing. **Nothing here changes what WordStrip predicts, what it inserts, or how keys are routed.**

Your settings carry over. If you were using Mica-inspired, Raycast Floating or visionOS-inspired, you get the
theme each was merged into rather than a reset to the default.

## Downloads

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `WordStrip-Setup-0.15.0.exe` | Installer, with Start Menu entries and an uninstaller. Recommended. |
| `WordStrip-0.15.0-portable.zip` | A single self-contained program. Unzip it and run `WordStrip.exe`. Nothing needs installing. |

## The bar now fits the text

The biggest change. WordStrip measures the line you are writing on and picks a size to match.

- **Small text** — Notepad at its default, a code editor, a dense note — gets a compact bar. On a real text
  box here it went from 48 px to 34 px.
- **An ordinary document** gets the size the bar has always been.
- **Large or scaled-up text** gets a roomier bar, without you having to go and change anything.

This is not the old thickness slider with a smaller number in it. Three sizes are drawn by hand, and the bar
blends between them: the type shrinks slowly so it stays readable, the padding gives up more of its share,
and the shadow gives up most — a small bar with a big shadow looks like a sticker stuck on the screen.

It also settles. Once it picks a size it stays there; a caret that twitches by a pixel, or disappears while
you select something, changes nothing.

**Settings → Appearance → Bar size** now offers **Automatic** (the default), **Compact**, **Standard** and
**Comfortable**. The three fixed ones ignore the text entirely. Settings shows what automatic actually chose,
in pixels, so you can check it rather than take it on trust. If you had previously moved the thickness
slider, you keep that size as a fixed density.

## Six themes

Three pairs were really one design each, so they merged; the room that freed went into two themes that are
nothing like the others.

| Theme | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Fluent Surface** | Windows-native, restrained translucency, tonal selection. The default. Acrylic and Mica merged here. |
| **Spatial Glass** | Light, luminous and the roomiest, with a soft capsule selection and no dividers. Apple Frosted and visionOS merged here. |
| **Command** | Dark, matte and compact, with a raised selection and fast motion. Fluent Depth and Raycast merged here. |
| **Material You** | Tonal and colour-forward, with a filled selection container. |
| **Paper & Ink** | New. A warm opaque sheet — no glass, no blur — where the selection is an ink underline and nothing else. Made for Notepad, Word and long-form writing. |
| **Terminal** | New. OLED black, monospaced, nearly square, instant, with a block cursor that knocks the text out of the selection. |

A theme is no longer just a colour scheme: each one differs in silhouette, density, candidate spacing,
selection language, typeface and motion as well. A check runs over the catalogue and fails if any two themes
drift within three differences of each other, or differ mainly by colour — the test being that you can still
tell them apart in a grey screenshot.

## Also

- The theme gallery shows all six with real miniature bars.
- The preview has its own density switch (Live / Compact / Standard / Roomy) that shows a size without
  changing your setting.
- Motion belongs to the theme now: Terminal is instant, Paper & Ink nearly still, Spatial Glass the most
  fluid. Your own motion setting still wins, and Off still means off everywhere.

## Known limitations

- Dark mode, High Contrast, transparency-off and display scales other than 150% have not been tried on real
  hardware for this release. The palettes are checked automatically, but if you run any of those, that is the
  most useful thing to look at.
- The monospaced Terminal theme uses Cascadia Code if you have it, then Cascadia Mono, then Consolas. It only
  ever changes the bar — never the application you are typing in.
- English only.
