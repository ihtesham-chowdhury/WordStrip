WordStrip — early preview
=========================

Thanks for testing this. It adds phone-keyboard-style word suggestions and autocorrect
to the normal Windows keyboard.


WHICH FILE DO I RUN?
--------------------

  WordStrip-Setup-*.exe       Installs it properly (Start Menu entry, optional start-with-Windows,
                              uninstall from Add/Remove Programs). No admin password needed.
                              Recommended.

  portable\WordStrip.exe      No installation. Just double-click it. Delete the file to remove it.

Either one works on its own. You do NOT need to install .NET or anything else.


"WINDOWS PROTECTED YOUR PC" — THIS IS EXPECTED
---------------------------------------------

The app isn't code-signed (a certificate costs a few hundred dollars a year), so Windows
shows a blue SmartScreen warning the first time.

To run it anyway:

  1. Click "More info"
  2. Click "Run anyway"

You'll only see this once.


HOW TO USE IT
-------------

1. Start WordStrip. It has no window — it lives in the system tray (bottom-right,
   you may need to click the "^" arrow to see it).

2. First launch takes about 10-20 seconds while it builds its spelling index.
   After that it starts quickly.

3. Open Notepad and start typing. A glass bar appears with suggested words.

     Tab            highlight the next word (hold it to scrub through them quickly)
     Space          insert the highlighted word      <-- the main one
     Esc            put the bar away
     Click a word   inserts it straight away, no need to press Tab first

   Using the keyboard, you press Tab first and then Space. If nothing is highlighted,
   Space is just a normal space, so your ordinary typing is never affected.

4. Between words, the bar PREDICTS WHAT COMES NEXT from the words you just typed,
   instead of showing the same handful of common words every time.

   Type "how are" and it offers: you, we, they
   Type "let me"  and it offers: see, go, have, know

   It also reorders suggestions while you're typing a word. After "I am", typing "wor"
   ranks "working" above "work", even though "work" is the more common word on its own.

   This is a statistical model built from public-domain books, running entirely on your
   machine. It has three words of context and no understanding of meaning, so it's good
   at ordinary English sentences and weak on technical or work jargon.

   FIXED IN 0.6: Tab now works on these predictions. Previously Tab only did anything
   while you were part-way through typing a word, so the predictions that appear right
   after you insert a word could only be clicked. Tab now cycles whenever the bar is
   showing anything, and Space inserts the highlighted word.

   The trade-off: while the bar is visible, Tab belongs to it and won't indent or move
   between fields. Press Esc to put the bar away and Tab goes back to normal until you
   type again. If that bothers you, turn off "keep the bar on screen between words".

   The bar STAYS ON SCREEN between words rather than vanishing every time you press
   space, the way a phone keyboard's suggestion row does.

5. NEW IN 0.6: YOUR OWN WORDS.  Settings -> Your words

   Add names, products, jargon - anything the built-in dictionary doesn't know. Added
   words get suggested as you type, AND they stop being autocorrected into something
   else. If you type "QNAP" and watch it turn into "snap", adding it fixes that.

   Capitalisation is kept exactly as you enter it, so "GitHub" stays "GitHub".

   You can import a list from a text file (one word per line) or export what you have.

6. NEW IN 0.6: LEARNING FROM YOUR TYPING.  Settings -> Learning

   This one is OFF unless you turn it on. It changes what the app records about you,
   which isn't something to switch on for someone without asking.

   With it on, WordStrip counts which words you use and which words tend to follow each
   other, and gradually leans suggestions towards how you actually write. Type
   "Northfield Data Systems" enough times and it starts predicting it.

   What it stores: counts of single words, word pairs and word triples. That's all.
   No sentences, no documents, no record of your keystrokes, and nothing at all from
   password fields. It never leaves your computer - there's no account, no upload and
   no telemetry of any kind.

   It also forgets: old counts fade over time, so something you typed a lot months ago
   won't dominate forever.

   The Learning page shows exactly how much has been learned, and there's one button to
   delete all of it.

7. Autocorrect fixes obvious misspellings when you finish a word
   ("recieve" becomes "receive"). Words you've added to Your words are left alone.

8. The bar goes away when you click somewhere else, press Esc, or leave the text box.

9. Right-click the tray icon -> Settings. There's a live preview, and you can change:

     - whether the bar stays on screen between words
     - how many words are shown (3 to 7)
     - your own word list (add / remove / import / export)
     - whether WordStrip learns from your typing, and clearing what it has learned
     - how thick the bar is
     - how thick/transparent the glass looks
     - how fast the animation is
     - where the bar sits: bottom of the screen, top, or following your text cursor


WHAT DOESN'T WORK YET — PLEASE DON'T REPORT THESE
-------------------------------------------------

* It only works in standard Windows text boxes: Notepad, and most desktop app text
  fields and dialogs.
* It does NOT work in Chrome, Edge, or any browser. It does not work in Electron apps
  (Slack, Discord, VS Code) or in Microsoft Office. This is the single biggest known
  gap and it's being worked on.
* English only.
* Predictions come from word statistics, not from understanding your sentence. The
  books they were built from are mostly 19th-century novels, so ordinary English is
  well covered and modern/technical phrasing is not.


PRIVACY
-------

Everything runs on your own machine. There is no internet connection, no account, and
no telemetry of any kind.

By default nothing you type is saved to disk at all. If you switch Learning on, what
gets saved is counts - how often you use each word, and which words follow which - and
never the text itself. No sentences, no documents, no keystroke log.

The app deliberately turns itself off inside password fields, and that applies to
learning too: it will not learn anything from a password box, or from any field it
can't positively identify as an ordinary text box.

Your files live in:  %LOCALAPPDATA%\WordStrip
You can open that folder, read them, and delete anything you like.

Note: uninstalling WordStrip deletes that folder, including your word list. Export it
first if you want to keep it.


WHAT FEEDBACK IS USEFUL
-----------------------

* Did the suggestions actually help, or get in the way?
* Are the next-word predictions any good? Which ones were obviously wrong or silly?
* Now that Tab belongs to the bar whenever it's visible - does that get in the way?
  Did you find yourself pressing Tab expecting to move between fields and getting a
  word instead? Esc releases it, but say so if that's a nuisance in practice.
* Does the bar staying on screen between words feel calmer than it flickering away
  after every word, or is it just clutter? Did you end up turning it off?
* Did you turn Learning on? Did suggestions noticeably improve, and how long did it
  take before you noticed?
* Is anything about what gets stored unclear or uncomfortable?
* Is Tab-then-Space comfortable, or would you prefer something else?
* Did text ever get inserted wrongly, or in the wrong colour/font? If so, which app?
* Does the animation feel right — too fast, too slow, or fine?
* Does the bar sit in a sensible place, or is it in the way?
* Anything that felt slow, ugly, or confusing.


TO UNINSTALL
------------

Installed version:  Settings -> Apps -> Installed apps -> WordStrip -> Uninstall
Portable version:   Right-click the tray icon -> Exit, then delete WordStrip.exe
